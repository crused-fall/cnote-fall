# Definition

## Basic definition of sequence

> **Sequence**
>  A sequence is a function $a:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$), denoted $\{a_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ or simply $a_n$.



> **Subsequence**
>  If $\{n_k\}$ is a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers, then $\{a_{n_k}\}$ is called a subsequence of $\{a_n\}$.



## Boundedness and Monotonicity

> **Upper bound, lower bound, bounded sequence**
>
> $\{a_n\}$ is **bounded above** if there exists $M$ such that $a_n \le M$ for all $n$.
>
> It is **bounded below** if there exists $m$ such that $a_n \ge m$.
>
> It is **bounded** if it is both bounded above and below.



> **Monotone sequence**
>
> $a_{n+1} \ge a_n$ for all $n$: monotone increasing (strict if $>$).
>
> $a_{n+1} \le a_n$ for all $n$: monotone decreasing (strict if $<$).



## Convergence and Limits

> **Convergence**
>  A sequence $\{a_n\}$ converges to $L\in\mathbb{R}$ if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $N$ such that
> $$
> |a_n - L| < \varepsilon \quad (n \ge N).
> $$
> Denoted $a_n \to L$.



> **Divergence**
>  A sequence that does not converge is said to diverge.



> **Convergence to infinity**
> $$
> a_n \to +\infty \quad \text{if for every } M>0,\; \exists N \text{ s.t. } a_n > M \text{ when } n\ge N.
> $$
> Similarly for $a_n \to -\infty$.



> **Limit point / cluster point**
>  If a subsequence $a_{n_k}\to L$, then $L$ is called a limit point of $\{a_n\}$.
>  The set of all limit points is the **limit set**.



> **Limsup and liminf**
> $$
> \limsup_{n\to\infty} a_n = \lim_{n\to\infty} \sup_{k\ge n} a_k, \qquad
> \liminf_{n\to\infty} a_n = \lim_{n\to\infty} \inf_{k\ge n} a_k.
> $$



## Special Types of Sequences

> **Definition 10 (Cauchy sequence)**
>  $\{a_n\}$ is Cauchy if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $N$ such that
> $$
> |a_m - a_n| < \varepsilon \qquad (m,n \ge N).
> $$



> **Definition 11 (Periodic sequence)**
>  If there exists $T\in\mathbb{N}$ such that $a_{n+T}=a_n$ for all $n$, the sequence is periodic.
>  The smallest such $T$ is the period.





## Asymptotic Relations

> **Equivalent infinitesimals**
>  If $a_n\to 0$, $b_n\to 0$, and
> $$
> \frac{a_n}{b_n} \to 1,
> $$
> then $a_n$ and $b_n$ are **asymptotically equivalent**, written $a_n \sim b_n$.



> **Big-O and small-o**
> $$
> a_n = o(b_n)\iff\frac{a_n}{b_n} \to 0
> $$
>
> $$
> a_n = O(b_n)\iff |a_n|\le C|b_n|\text{ for sufficiently large } n
> $$



## Series-Related Definitions (Sequence-Based)

> **Sequence of partial sums**
>  For a series $\sum a_n$, the partial sums
> $$
> s_n = \sum_{k=1}^n a_k
> $$
> form a sequence $\{s_n\}$.
>  The series converges if and only if $\{s_n\}$ converges.
